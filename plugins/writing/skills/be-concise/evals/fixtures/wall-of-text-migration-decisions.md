Following on from the architecture sync: we cannot land the tenant split until you pick a lane on four things, and they interact, so picking them in isolation will not work. First, the shard key. Either `tenant_id` alone, which keeps every tenant on one shard and makes the noisy-neighbour problem worse for the three tenants over 40 GB, or the composite `(tenant_id, region)`, which spreads those three but breaks the single-shard guarantee that the reporting rollups currently rely on, so the rollups would move to the fan-out reader and inherit its 12-second p99. Second, the cutover mode. Dual-write with a read-repair window means we carry both schemas for roughly six weeks and accept that any row written during the window can be repaired twice, which the audit log will show as two distinct mutations with the same actor; the alternative, a freeze-and-copy over a two-hour maintenance window, avoids all of that but needs sign-off from the two enterprise accounts whose contracts cap planned downtime at 30 minutes per quarter, and we have already used 25 of those minutes in Q3. Third, what happens to the legacy `tenant_meta` table, which the billing exporter still reads directly even though it was supposed to be retired in the 3.x line: we either keep it as a view over the new tables, which costs us a join on the exporter's hot path and roughly doubles its run time, or we change the exporter, which is owned by a team that has no capacity until the middle of November and would need the change specced before then. Fourth, and this one is genuinely time-boxed, the region pin: if we do not decide before the provider's capacity reservation window closes on 2026-10-02 we lose the reserved instances in eu-west and fall back to on-demand pricing for the whole migration, which is about 18 thousand more for the quarter. My own view is composite key, dual-write, keep `tenant_meta` as a view for now, and pin the region this week, but the shard key and the rollup latency question is the one I am least sure about and I would rather you looked at it properly than agreed with me.
