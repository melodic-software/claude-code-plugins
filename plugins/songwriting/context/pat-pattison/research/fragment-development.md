# Fragment Development — Growing Partial Material

The writer has a partial line, hook fragment, half a verse, or scrap of
language but doesn't know how to develop it. This file routes fragment →
draft.

Distinct from:

- `brainstorm.md` — no material yet
- `idea-to-title.md` — has a seed but no language yet
- `demo-review.md` — has a more complete draft to diagnose

## When to load

Trigger phrases: "I have this line", "I have a hook idea", "I have a half-
verse", "this phrase keeps coming back to me", "this won't grow", "where do
I take this", "I have a chorus but no verses", "I have a verse but no
chorus", "this fragment is stuck".

## Step 1 — Inventory the fragment

Read the fragment aloud. Identify:

- **Is it complete-shaped or partial-shaped?**
  - Complete: a full line / a full couplet / a chorus stanza
  - Partial: a hook word / a half-line / an opening clause
- **What's its structural position-tendency?** Does it sound like a
  - verse opener
  - verse close (refrain candidate)
  - chorus first line
  - chorus last line
  - bridge release
  - hook
  - intro / coda
- **What's strong about it?** (image, sound, rhythm, twist, specificity)
- **What's it missing?** (context, image, who's speaking, why now)

Don't try to "fix" the fragment yet. Inventory first.

## Step 2 — What does the fragment imply?

Strong fragments imply more than they say. Mine the implications:

- **Speaker** — who says this? what's their stake?
- **Audience** — to whom? a lover, an enemy, the self, no one?
- **Time** — when? present, past, ongoing, future, conditional?
- **Place** — where? does the language smell of a setting?
- **Era** — what year / decade / season does the diction belong to?
- **Tone** — controlled, raw, ironic, sincere, accusatory, resigned?
- **Genre proximity** — country? folk? indie rock? hip-hop? cabaret?
- **What's just happened off-page** — the precipitating moment

Surface the implications. The writer may not have realized what the
fragment is already telling.

## Step 3 — Object-write the implied world

Once implications are surfaced, run a 10-minute object-write on the world
the fragment implies. Per `object-writing.md`.

- If fragment implies a Tennessee bar, object-write that bar.
- If fragment implies a 1996 telephone, object-write that phone.
- If fragment implies an unsent letter, object-write the writing of it.

The object-write generates vocabulary, images, verbs, and details that can
support the fragment outward.

## Step 4 — Identify the fragment's structural role

Based on Step 1's position-tendency + Step 2's implications, the fragment
fits one of these structural roles:

| Role | What it is | Build outward by... |
|---|---|---|
| Hook | the title or a chorus refrain line | locating title in central section; building chorus around it; verses point toward it |
| Chorus line | one line of a chorus stanza | drafting the other chorus lines (per `repetition.md` repaintability); building verses that prepare it |
| Verse opener | strong first line of a verse | drafting the verse's hot-spot last line (the trigger position per `verse-development.md`); then filling middle |
| Verse closer / refrain | what each verse closes with | building verses that lead to it; deciding if it's a refrain (*Essential Guide to Lyric Form and Structure* (1991), Chapter 5) |
| Bridge release | the perspective-shift line | locating verses + chorus first; bridge designed to deliver this line |
| Pre-chorus / TB | a setup line | drafting verse + chorus first; bringing this line in as the connector |
| Stand-alone scrap | strong language without a position yet | shelf it; let it find its position during draft |

If position is unclear, the fragment may not be ready to develop. Shelf
and revisit later — strong fragments usually declare their position when
the surrounding context develops.

## Step 5 — Build outward (working backwards from strong material)

Pat's principle: build the central section first. If the fragment IS the
central section's seed, draft outward from it. If the fragment is a verse
piece, locate the central section first, then draft the fragment's verse.

Per `verse-development.md`:

- **Power positions** — line 1 of section, last line of section, the line
  before the chorus (trigger line). Strong material belongs in power
  positions.
- **Trigger line** — the line before the chorus, hands meaning into the
  chorus. If the fragment can be a trigger line, lock it there.

Draft outward in order:

1. Identify the song's central section (chorus or refrain). Where does the
   fragment belong relative to it?
2. Draft the central section if not yet present (chorus / refrain).
3. Draft the section the fragment belongs in (verse / bridge / etc.).
4. Draft the remaining sections, supporting the fragment's role.
5. Apply prosody pass (per `prosody.md`).
6. Apply rhyme pass (per `rhyme-strategy.md`).

## Step 6 — Pre-lock the fragment

Run `audit-checklist.md` per-line checklist on the fragment in its new
context:

- Stress / meter (Pat's pattern matching)
- Rhyme stability (if in rhyme position)
- Hot-spot content (if in power position)
- POV consistency
- Image specificity
- Prosody fit

Most fragments survive their original form; some get sharpened during the
build. A fragment that wants to change as the song develops is a healthy
fragment — Pat: every revision is an option, not a betrayal.

## When the fragment resists development

If the fragment won't grow:

- Object-write the implied world for 20 minutes (double the usual). The
  writer may not have enough material yet.
- Try the fragment in a different position (verse opener → chorus close).
  Position changes meaning.
- Try the fragment in a different POV. First → second often unsticks.
- Try the fragment in a different tense. Present → past or past → present.
- Set it aside for a week. Some fragments are seeds for the next song, not
  this one.

## Common failure modes

| Failure | Recovery |
|---|---|
| Writer wants to draft straight from fragment | back up; do Steps 1-3 first |
| Fragment is brilliant in isolation, generic in context | the world isn't developed enough; deeper object-writing |
| Fragment forces unnatural rhymes | run `rhyme-generation.md` on the stressed vowel; if zero clean rhymes, the fragment may be in the wrong form (lives-once vs repeats) |
| Multiple fragments compete | the song probably wants to develop around ONE; the others go to `ideation/fragments.md` for later |
| Fragment requires a title that doesn't fit | title and fragment are connected — try alternate titles per `idea-to-title.md` |

## Artifact pattern

The fragment + Step 1/2 inventory + Step 3 object-write material land in
`songwriting/songs/<slug>/ideation/fragments.md` (per the SKILL.md
"Artifact Persistence" layout).

Once development begins, fragment material migrates to `LYRIC.md` and
working variations to `variations/<section>.md`.
