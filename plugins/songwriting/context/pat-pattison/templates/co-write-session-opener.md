# Co-Write Session Opener

Use this prompt at the start of a co-writing session. Emits Pat
Pattison's No-Free-Zone rules as a printable opener, plus the four
session-protocol rules. Print it, post it, or read it aloud at the
top of the session.

```text
=== Co-Write Session Opener — Pat Pattison's No-Free-Zone ===

Date: <date>
Co-writers: <names>
Working title or seed: <seed>
Session goal: <draft a verse | finish chorus | block out form | etc.>

Four rules — say these aloud at the open:

1. No "no."
   Say everything that comes to your head. No matter how dumb. Silence
   in the room means "keep going." A spoken "yes" advances the line.
   Defensiveness kills the song.

2. Write crap. Lots of it. All the time. The more the better.
   The good ideas hide inside the bad ones. We dig them out later.

3. Stay inside the song and inside the characters.
   No meta-talk in the room. No "what are we doing", "is this any
   good", "this isn't working for me". Speak as the song. If the
   speaker would not say it, the speaker doesn't say it now.

4. No technical talk while drafting.
   No "that rhyme is family", "that's a deceptive closure", "that's
   tetrameter". Technical talk is for the revision pass, after the
   draft exists.

Session structure:

A. Seed warmup (5-10 minutes)
   - Both writers object-write the same seed silently. No discussion
     during the write. 5-10 minutes.

B. Compare material (10 minutes)
   - Read pages aloud. Underline the strongest sensory images,
     verbs, body details, possible titles. No critique of the
     writing — only mining.

C. Central section first (40-60 minutes)
   - Identify whether the song wants a chorus, refrain, or AABA
     central. Draft the central section together. Trade lines.
     Apply the four rules above. Do not start a verse until the
     central section has its first draft.

D. Outer sections by trading (40-60 minutes)
   - One writer drafts verse 1; the other drafts verse 2. Or
     alternate lines. Or write parallel drafts and reconcile.

E. Cliche check at every section boundary (5 minutes per boundary)
   - Fresh-eyes pass at each section break. Flag predictable
     rhymes, generic labels, convenient abstractions. Mark — do
     not fix yet.

F. Stability check (10 minutes)
   - One pass through the draft asking "does the song's motion
     match its central emotion?" See [stable / unstable](../research/stable-unstable-meta.md).

G. Wrap (10 minutes)
   - Record the demo (rough phone capture is fine).
   - Confirm who takes the home revision and when the next session is.
   - Each writer notes one thing they want to revisit.

After the session, each writer revises solo. Co-writing surfaces
material; revision is solitary work.

Anti-patterns to flag during the session:

- A writer keeps explaining their idea instead of writing it. Stop.
  Write it down — even half-formed — and let the partner build on it.
- The room goes quiet for more than 2 minutes. Silence stalls.
  Re-read the last line aloud. Write the next thing that comes.
- Technical critique appears mid-draft. Note it for revision. Keep
  drafting.
- One writer takes over. Trade the pen literally (or the keyboard).
- Defensiveness about a line. Apply rule 1. Write the alternative
  without rejecting the original — leave both on the page; choose
  later.
- The pair starts diagnosing the song before the draft exists.
  Drafting and diagnosing are different sessions.
```

Notes:

- The opener is read aloud at session start by either writer.
- Print or text the rules to both writers ahead of time.
- The protocol is one suggested shape; adapt to the room.
- The protocol is co-write only; it is not a solo-writing
  discipline.
