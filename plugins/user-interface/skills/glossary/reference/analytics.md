# Analytics terms

How design decisions are measured. Each entry gives the term, a definition, and the line between it
and the term most often confused with it.

## Analysis: `analysis`

### A/B test: `a-b-test`

Two design variants running simultaneously to measure which performs better. Validates decisions
with real behavior rather than internal opinion. Results are only as good as the metric being
measured.

**Versus:** a usability test watches a few people use one design; an A/B test measures many people
on two variants.

### Session recording: `session-recording`

Video of individual user sessions through a product. Shows exactly where people hesitate, get
confused, or give up. More specific than a heatmap, and harder to ignore.

**Versus:** a heatmap aggregates many sessions into one picture; a session recording replays one.

### Conversion: `conversion`

The moment a visitor takes the desired action. Design affects it through clarity, trust, friction
reduction, and the quality of the CTA.

**Versus:** retention is coming back later; conversion is taking the desired action now.

### Retention: `retention`

How many users come back after their first visit. A product can have strong acquisition and terrible
retention. Design affects both, but retention is the harder and more important problem.

**Versus:** churn is the users who stop; retention is the users who come back. Conversion is the
first desired action.

### NPS: `nps`

Net Promoter Score. A measure of how likely users are to recommend a product, on a scale of 0 to 10.
A blunt instrument, but widely used as a rough signal of overall satisfaction.

**Versus:** a satisfaction score asks how happy users are; NPS asks how likely they are to
recommend.

### Heatmap: `heatmap`

A visualization of where users click, tap, or scroll on a page. Shows what draws attention and what
gets ignored. Useful for spotting affordance problems and finding content nobody reaches.

**Versus:** a session recording replays one person; a heatmap aggregates clicks, taps or scrolls
from many.

### Funnel: `funnel`

The path users take toward a conversion goal: signing up, purchasing, completing a task. Drop-off at
any step is a design problem. Most funnels lose the majority of users before the end.

**Versus:** conversion is the goal step; the funnel is the path of steps toward it.

### Bounce rate: `bounce-rate`

The percentage of visitors who leave without taking any action. High bounce rates on key pages often
point to a mismatch between what users expected and what they found.

**Versus:** churn is users who stop using the product over time; bounce rate is visitors who leave a
page without acting.

### Churn: `churn`

Users who stop using the product. The inverse of retention. High churn means the product is not
delivering enough value to keep people coming back.

**Versus:** bounce rate is one visit ending without action; churn is a user leaving the product.

### Scroll depth: `scroll-depth`

How far down a page users actually read. Most don't get to the bottom. Content and CTAs buried below
the fold may be seen by far fewer people than assumed.

**Versus:** a heatmap shows where people click or tap; scroll depth shows how far down they read.
